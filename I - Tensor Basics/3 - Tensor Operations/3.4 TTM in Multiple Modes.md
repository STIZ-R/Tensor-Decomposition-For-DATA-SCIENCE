# 3.4 TTM in Multiple Modes

When we compute the TTMs, the order of the multiplications does not matter.
Indeed,
> **Proposition (TTM Order)** <br>
> *Let* $\mathbf{x}$ *be a tensor such that mode* $k$ *is of size* $m$ *and mode* $l$ *is of size* $n$*. *Then, for any* $\mathbf{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times m}$ *and* $\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times n}$ *with* $k \neq l$, *whe have,* <br>
> $( \mathbf{x} \times_k \mathbf{U}) \times_l \mathbf{V} = (\mathbf{x} \times_l \mathbf{V}) \times_k \mathbf{U}.$



## 3.4.1 Multi-TTM for 3-way Tensors

We learnt that for a tensor $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n \times p}$, the expression is
$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{x} \times_1 \mathbf{U} \times_2 \mathbf{V} \times_3 \mathbf{W}$ <br> with $\mathbf{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times m}, \mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times n} \text{ and } \mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{s \times p}$. <br>
Then the size of $\mathbf{y}$ became $q \times r \times s$. <br>
Each matrix transforms the tensor along one mode: mode-1, mode-2, and mode-3 respectively. <br>
This operation is useful to project or compress data along different dimensions such as *time*, *location*, or *season* for example.

<br>

Let take a tensor $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{ \text{cities} \times \text{days} \times \text{sensors}}$: <br>
We have $m = 10$ cities; $n = 365$ days; $p = 5$ sensors. <br>
Then **Mode-1** (cities): $\mathbf{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 10}$ -> 10 cities in 3 states. <br>
**Mode-2** (days): $\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{12 \times 365}$ -> 365 days in 12 months. <br>
**Mode-3** (sensors): $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 5}$ -> 5 sensors in 2 latent variables. <br>
We can now express  $\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 12 \times 2}$ as aggregated measurements by *region*, by *month*, and by *latent sensor type*.

We can use the Kronecker product to express the **vectorization** and **unfoldings** as follows: <br>
> **Proposition (Mutli-TTM Vectorization and Mode-*k* Unfolding, 3-way)** <br>
> *Let* $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n \times p}, \mathbf{U} \in \mathbb{q \times m}, \mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times n} \text{ and } \mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{s \times p}$ *so that* $\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times r \time s}.$ <<br>
> *The followoing are equivalent:* <br>
> $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{x} \times_1 \mathbf{U} \times_2 \mathbf{V} \times_3 \mathbf{W},$ <br>
> $\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{y}) = (\mathbf{W} \otimes \mathbf{V} \otimes \mathbf{U}) \mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{x}),$ <br>
