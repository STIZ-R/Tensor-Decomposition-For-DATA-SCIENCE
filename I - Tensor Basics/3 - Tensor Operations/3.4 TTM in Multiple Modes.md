# 3.4 TTM in Multiple Modes

When we compute the TTMs, the order of the multiplications does not matter.
Indeed,
> **Proposition (TTM Order)** <br>
> *Let* $\mathbf{x}$ *be a tensor such that mode* $k$ *is of size* $m$ *and mode* $l$ *is of size* $n$*. *Then, for any* $\mathbf{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times m}$ *and* $\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times n}$ *with* $k \neq l$, *whe have,* <br>
> $( \mathbf{x} \times_k \mathbf{U}) \times_l \mathbf{V} = (\mathbf{x} \times_l \mathbf{V}) \times_k \mathbf{U}.$



## 3.4.1 Multi-TTM for 3-way Tensors

We learnt that for a tensor $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n \times p}$, the expression is
$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{x} \times_1 \mathbf{U} \times_2 \mathbf{V} \times_3 \mathbf{W}$ <br> with $\mathbf{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times m}, \mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times n} \text{ and } \mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{s \times p}$. <br>
Then the size of $\mathbf{y}$ became $q \times r \times s$. <br>
Each matrix transforms the tensor along one mode: mode-1, mode-2, and mode-3 respectively. <br>
This operation is useful to project or compress data along different dimensions such as *time*, *location*, or *season* for example.
