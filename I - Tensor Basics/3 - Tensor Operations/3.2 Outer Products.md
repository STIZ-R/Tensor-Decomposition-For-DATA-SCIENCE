# 3.2 Outer Products

## 3.2.1 Outer Product of Three Vectors

Unlike the inner product, the outer product of three vectors generates a 3-way tensor.

> **Definition (Outer Product of Three Vectors)** <br>
> The **outer product** of vectors $a \in \mathbb{R}^m, b \in \mathbb{R}^n, c \in \mathbb{R}^p$ is denoted $a \circ b \circ c$ and produces an $m \times n \times p$ tensor, such that element $(i,j,k)$ equals $a_ib_jc_k$:
![Figure Outer Product](../../image/Figure_OuterProduct.jpg)

<br>

For the computational complexity of the outer product of vectors of length $m$, $n$ and $p$ is the product of sizes: $\mathcal{O}(mnp)$.

<br>

> **Definition (Rank-1 3-way Tensor)** <br>
> A 3-way Tnsor $\mathbf{x}$ is **rank 1** if it can be expressed as a vector outer product, *i.e.*, there exist vectors $\textbf{a, b, c}$ such that $\mathbf{x} = \textbf{a} \circ \textbf{b} \circ \textbf{c}$.   

<br>

Let show that with a simple example;

<br>

Since the Kronecker product produces a vector with reverse linearization, you need to use the reverse Kronecker product to have it equal to the outer product.

## 3.2.2 Outer Product of *d* Vectors

> **Definition (Outer Product of *d* Vectors)** <br>
> The **outer product** of *d* vectors $a_k \in \mathbb{R}^{n_k}$ for all $k \in [d]$ is a *d*-way tensor denoted $a_1 \circ a_2 \circ \times \circ a_d$ of size $n_1 \times n_2 \times \dots \times n_d$, such that element $(i_1, i_2, \dots, i_d)$ is given by $\prod_{k=1}^da_k(i_k).$

For the computational complexity of the outer product of vectors of length $N = n_1 \times n_2 \times \dots \times n_k$ is the product of sizes: $\mathcal{O}(N)$.

> **Definition (Rank-1 *d*-way Tensor)** <br>
> A *d*-way tensor $\mathbf{X}$ is **rank 1** if it can be written as an outer product of *d* vectors. In other words, there exists vectors $\\{a_k\\}_{k=1}^d$ such that $\mathbf{x} = a_1 \circ a_2 \circ \dots \circ a_d.$

We can write the reshaping matrix of $\mathbf{X_{(\mathfrak{R}\times \mathfrak{C})}} = (a_{\mathfrak{r_{\delta}}} \otimes \dots \otimes a_{\mathfrak{r_{1}}})(a_{\mathfrak{c_{d - \delta}}} \otimes \dots \otimes a_{\mathfrak{r_{1}}})^T$ where $\mathfrak{R} = (\mathfrak{r_1}, \dots, \mathfrak{r_{\delta}})$ and $\mathfrak{C} = (\mathfrak{c_1}, \dots, \mathfrak{c_{d - \delta}})$ is an ordered partitioning of $[d].$ 

<br>

We can take the following example:
```math
\begin{aligned}
a = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad b = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad c=\begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} \qquad \text{ and } d=\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 5\end{bmatrix}. \\[8pt]
\text{Let R = (3,1) and C = (2,4). Then :} \\[8pt]
(a \otimes c)(d \otimes b)^T = \begin{bmatrix} 20 \\ 12 \\ 10 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix} \qquad \begin{bmatrix} 9 & 2 & 15 & 5 \end{bmatrix} \qquad = \begin{bmatrix} 180 & 60 & 300 & 100 \\ 108 & 36 & 180 & 60 \\ 90 & 30 & 150 & 50 \\ 54 & 18 & 90 & 30 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{X_{(\mathfrak{R} \times \mathfrak{C})}}.
\end{aligned}
```
